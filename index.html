The problem

The paper was the only proof that someone had received their certificate, and that proof was at risk.

What was going wrong

  • During an El Niño season, the strongest hailstorm in years (hail the size of tennis balls) broke parts of the office roof. Water leaked in and soaked and stained the sheets kept closest at hand, so the proof of delivery was at risk.
  • To check if someone had picked up a certificate, staff flipped through sheets by hand: about 3 minutes for the current year, twice as long or more if the sheet was already archived.
  • Records were messy: signatures spanning two rows, wrong years, notes in the margins, small signed slips taped to the back.

What the owners asked for

  • Don’t change how we work. People keep signing on paper, no on-screen signatures yet.
  • Make it a standalone system, independent from all the company’s existing software, not just the payments system. The goal was to go from paper sheets stored in a cabinet to fully digital records.
  • No new software costs.

How it works now

Signing still happens on paper. The only new step is taking a photo with a phone.

  1. Sign a paper slip

    A redesigned slip with boxes for the course code and person number, so every photo identifies itself.

  2. Take a photo

    With any phone, from the web app. Works on phone and PC, no Google account needed.

  3. Photo is shrunk

    Compressed in the browser from 3–5 MB to about 250 KB (over 90% smaller).

  4. Saved and named automatically

    Stored in a Drive folder by year and course, named code - no. - name - date.

  5. Register updated

    The spreadsheet (one tab per course, same layout as the paper sheet) gets a “View signature” link.

  6. Search in seconds

    Type a name or ID number and see every course and year, with the signature photo.

It also covers the other ways a certificate leaves the office: picked up by someone else, sent by courier (with tracking number), or signed on an old sheet. Records are never deleted. A wrong entry is marked “void” and its photo is kept.

Moving the paper archive into the system

The largest part of the project. 2024–2026 is done: ~100 scanned pages, 37 courses and ~1,550 records, with signed slips taped to the back of many sheets. Now I’m applying the same process to every year back to 2018.

1. Scan

Front (the list) and back (taped slips) of every sheet, year by year.

2. Transcribe

Names, ID numbers, dates and how each certificate was handed over.

3. Validate

Automatic checks on numbering, ID format and dates, plus a list of doubtful cases.

4. Import

One import created each course with its records, linked to the right page photo.

End to end: from talking with the team to handing over a working tool and a short guide.

  • Process mapping with users
  • Solution design within business constraints

  • Validation & quality control

  • Personal-data privacy
Technical details
  • Backend in Google Apps Script (JavaScript V8), with a lock so two people can’t save at the same time.
  • Frontend in plain HTML/CSS/JavaScript, no libraries, with the company’s colors.
  • One spreadsheet per year, one tab per course. The course code (e.g. 26-05) sets the year, tab and photo folder.
  • Password access with lockout after failed attempts and 6-hour sessions.
  • Built and tested first as a prototype with fictional data, then moved to the company account. Developed locally with clasp and git.
  • The app belongs 100% to the company and keeps working without me.
  • After the migration, all temporary files with personal data were deleted.
